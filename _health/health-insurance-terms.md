Insurance terms can make two plans that look similar cost very different amounts. These definitions use common US terms, but many apply to private insurance in other countries too.

## The key terms

**Premium** — what you pay, usually monthly, to keep your plan active, whether or not you use care.

**Deductible** — how much you pay for covered services each year before your plan starts sharing costs. Some preventive services are covered before the deductible.

**Copayment (copay)** — a fixed amount you pay for a service, such as $30 for a doctor visit or $10 for a prescription.

**Coinsurance** — your share of the cost as a percentage after you meet the deductible, such as 20%.

**Out-of-pocket maximum** — the most you pay in a year for covered, in-network care. After you reach it, the plan pays 100% of covered services for the rest of the year. Premiums do not count toward it.

**Network** — the doctors, hospitals and pharmacies your plan has contracts with. Out-of-network care usually costs more, and some plans do not cover it at all.

**Prior authorisation** — approval the plan requires before certain tests, procedures or medicines. Without it, the plan may refuse to pay.

**Explanation of benefits (EOB)** — a statement from your insurer showing what was billed, what the plan paid and what you may owe. It is not a bill.

**HSA and FSA** — accounts that let you pay for eligible health costs with pre-tax money. A health savings account goes with high-deductible plans and the money rolls over; a flexible spending account usually has use-it-or-lose-it rules.

## A worked example

Suppose your plan has a $1,500 deductible, 20% coinsurance and a $5,000 out-of-pocket maximum, and you need a $6,000 procedure in network early in the year.

| Step | You pay |
|---|---|
| First $1,500 (deductible) | $1,500 |
| 20% of the remaining $4,500 | $900 |
| **Total for this procedure** | **$2,400** |

You would still have $2,600 left before reaching your $5,000 out-of-pocket maximum for the year.

## Avoiding surprise bills

- Confirm that the clinic, the clinician and any lab or imaging center are all in network.
- Ask whether prior authorisation is needed, and who requests it.
- Ask for a written cost estimate before planned care. Self-pay patients can often ask for a cash price.
- Compare each bill with your EOB, and question anything you do not recognise.
- If you cannot afford a bill, ask about payment plans or financial assistance before it goes to collections.

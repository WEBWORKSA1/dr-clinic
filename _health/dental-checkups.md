Oral diseases such as tooth decay and gum disease affect billions of people and are largely preventable. Regular checkups catch problems early, when treatment is simpler and cheaper.

## How often should I go?

There is no single rule. Your dentist should recommend an interval based on your oral health and risk — UK NICE guidance suggests between 3 months and 2 years for adults. People with gum disease, frequent decay, diabetes or who smoke often need more frequent visits. Many people with healthy mouths are seen once or twice a year.

## What happens at a checkup

- Questions about your health, medicines and any problems
- An examination of teeth, gums, tongue and mouth, including checks for signs of oral cancer
- X-rays if needed
- Cleaning to remove plaque and tartar, often by a dental hygienist
- Advice on brushing, cleaning between teeth, diet and smoking
- A written treatment plan with costs if you need work done

## Daily habits that protect your teeth

- Brush twice a day for about two minutes with fluoride toothpaste. Spit out after brushing rather than rinsing, so the fluoride keeps working.
- Clean between your teeth daily with floss or interdental brushes.
- Cut down on how often you have sugary foods and drinks — frequency matters as much as amount.
- Do not smoke or use tobacco, and limit alcohol.
- Replace your toothbrush every three months.

## See a dentist soon if you notice

- Bleeding gums when brushing
- Toothache, or sensitivity that lasts
- A broken or loose tooth, or a lost filling
- Bad breath that does not go away
- A mouth ulcer, white or red patch, or lump that lasts more than three weeks

<div class="callout warn" markdown="1">
**Get emergency care** for facial or neck swelling with fever, swelling that affects your breathing, swallowing or eye, heavy bleeding after an extraction that will not stop with pressure, or a serious mouth injury. If an adult tooth is knocked out, hold it by the crown, keep it in milk or saliva and see a dentist immediately — ideally within an hour.

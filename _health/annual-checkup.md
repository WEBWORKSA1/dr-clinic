A routine checkup — sometimes called a physical or wellness visit — is a chance to catch problems early and plan how to stay healthy. Recommendations differ between countries and individuals, but most visits cover similar ground.

## What usually happens

- **History update:** new symptoms, medicines, allergies, family history, work, sleep, mood, alcohol, smoking or vaping, and activity.
- **Measurements:** blood pressure, weight, and often waist size and heart rate.
- **Physical exam:** focused on your age and concerns.
- **Screening decisions:** which tests are due for you.
- **Vaccines:** reviewing what is due.
- **Planning:** goals and follow-up for any conditions.

## Common screening topics

Examples from US Preventive Services Task Force recommendations for average-risk adults (other countries use similar but not identical schedules):

- **Blood pressure:** all adults, at least every few years and yearly from age 40 or if at higher risk.
- **Diabetes and prediabetes:** adults 35 to 70 who have overweight or obesity.
- **Cholesterol and heart risk:** usually discussed from middle age, earlier with risk factors.
- **Cervical cancer:** from age 21 to 65, at intervals depending on the test used.
- **Breast cancer:** mammography every two years from age 40 to 74.
- **Colorectal cancer:** from age 45 to 75, with several test options.
- **Lung cancer:** yearly low-dose CT for some people aged 50 to 80 with a significant smoking history.
- **Depression and anxiety:** screening questions for adults.

If you have a strong family history or other risk factors, screening may start earlier or happen more often.

## Vaccines to review

Adults commonly need a yearly flu vaccine, tetanus boosters, and age-based vaccines such as shingles and pneumococcal vaccines, plus others depending on health, pregnancy, travel and work. Bring your vaccination record if you have one.

## How to prepare

1. List any symptoms or concerns, even small ones — and put the most important first.
2. Bring your [medicine list](/tools/medication-schedule/) and any home readings.
3. Update your family history: heart disease, diabetes, cancers and the ages they were diagnosed.
4. Check which tests your plan covers as preventive care.
5. Use our [visit prep builder](/tools/visit-prep/) and choose “Annual checkup”.

## Questions worth asking

- Which screenings are due for me, and why?
- How are my numbers trending compared with last time?
- What is the single most useful change I could make this year?

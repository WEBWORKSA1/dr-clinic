A primary care clinician is the person you see first for most health needs. They handle checkups and screening, treat everyday illnesses, manage long-term conditions such as high blood pressure or diabetes, and refer you to specialists when needed. Seeing the same clinician over time means someone knows your history — which tends to make care safer and more efficient.

## Types of primary care clinicians

- **Family medicine doctors (GPs)** care for people of all ages, from babies to older adults.
- **Internal medicine doctors (internists)** focus on adults, often those with several long-term conditions.
- **Pediatricians** care for children and teenagers.
- **Nurse practitioners and physician assistants** provide much of the same primary care in many practices and are often easier to book.
- **Geriatricians** specialise in older adults with complex needs.

## Practical checks first

1. **Are they accepting new patients?** Call or check the practice website.
2. **Are they covered?** Confirm they are in your insurance network or the right public system for your address.
3. **Is it convenient?** Location, opening hours, parking or public transport, and whether they offer telehealth.
4. **How do you reach them?** Ask about same-day appointments, online messaging, prescription refills and an out-of-hours line.
5. **Which hospital do they use?** If you have a preferred hospital, check their affiliations.

## Questions worth asking

- How long are routine appointments, and how far ahead do they book?
- Can I message the care team between visits?
- Who covers when my doctor is away?
- Do you have experience with my condition, or with patients like me?
- Which languages do staff speak, and are interpreters available?

## Checking credentials

Most countries have a public register of licensed doctors and nurses run by the medical or nursing regulator. It takes a minute to confirm that a clinician is licensed and has no restrictions. Board certification or fellowship in family or internal medicine shows additional training.

## The first visit

Treat your first appointment as a two-way interview. Bring your medicine list, past records if you have them, and your questions. Notice whether the clinician listens without rushing, explains things clearly and involves you in decisions. If it does not feel right, it is reasonable to try someone else.

## Switching doctors

You can usually change primary care clinicians at any time. Ask your old practice to transfer your records, and book a first appointment with the new one before you run out of any regular prescriptions.

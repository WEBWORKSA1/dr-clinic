Physiotherapists (physical therapists) are licensed health professionals who assess and treat problems with movement and function. They use exercise, hands-on treatment and education to reduce pain and help you get back to what you want to do.

## What physiotherapists treat

- Back and neck pain
- Sports injuries, sprains and strains
- Knee, hip and shoulder problems, including arthritis
- Recovery after surgery such as joint replacement or ligament repair
- Balance problems and falls prevention
- Recovery after stroke and other neurological conditions
- Pelvic floor problems, including during and after pregnancy
- Breathing problems in some lung conditions

## Do I need a referral?

It depends on where you live and how you pay. Many countries and all US states allow some form of direct access, meaning you can book without a doctor's referral, though some insurance plans still require one. Check with your insurer or health system first.

## Your first appointment

1. **Conversation:** your symptoms, how they started, what makes them better or worse, and your goals.
2. **Assessment:** how you move, your strength, flexibility and balance.
3. **Explanation:** what is likely going on, in plain language.
4. **Plan:** treatment in the clinic and exercises to do at home, with a timeline.

Wear comfortable clothes that let the physio see and move the affected area.

## How many sessions?

It varies widely. Some problems need two or three visits with a good home program; others, such as rehabilitation after surgery, need weeks or months. Ask at the start how many sessions they expect and how you will both judge progress.

## Choosing a physiotherapist

- Check registration with your national physiotherapy regulator.
- Ask about experience with your specific problem — for example sports, pelvic health or neurological rehabilitation.
- Prefer a clinician who gives you an active home program, not only passive treatments.
- Ask about cost per session and cancellation policies.

<div class="callout" markdown="1">
See a doctor first, or urgently, if you have pain after a serious injury, a possible fracture, numbness or weakness that is getting worse, unexplained fever or weight loss, or loss of bladder or bowel control.
</div>

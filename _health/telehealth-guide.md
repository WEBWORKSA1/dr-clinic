Telehealth means getting care at a distance — usually by video, sometimes by phone or secure messaging. It saves travel, time off work and waiting rooms, and for many problems it is just as effective as an in-person visit.

## What telehealth handles well

- Colds, sinus symptoms, mild flu-like illness and many rashes
- Urinary symptoms in otherwise healthy adults (depending on local rules)
- Medication questions, side effects and many prescription renewals
- Follow-ups for long-term conditions, especially with home readings
- Mental-health care, including talking therapy and psychiatry follow-ups
- Reviewing test results and planning next steps

## When you need to be seen in person

Some problems need hands-on examination, tests or treatment that cannot happen over video — for example a suspected broken bone, deep wounds, ear infections that need the eardrum checked, abdominal pain, or anything requiring blood tests the same day. A good telehealth clinician will tell you when an in-person visit is needed.

<div class="callout warn" markdown="1">
Telehealth is **not** for emergencies. For chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs, severe bleeding or other emergencies, call your emergency number.
</div>

## How to prepare

1. **Test your setup.** Charge your device, check the app or link, and allow camera and microphone access.
2. **Find a quiet, private, well-lit space.** Face the light so the clinician can see you clearly.
3. **Have information ready.** Medicine list, allergies, symptom timeline and your questions. Our [visit prep builder](/tools/visit-prep/) puts these on one page.
4. **Gather home readings.** Temperature, blood pressure, weight or blood sugar if relevant. Our [blood pressure log](/tools/blood-pressure/) keeps recent readings on your device.
5. **Know your pharmacy.** Have its name and address ready for prescriptions.
6. **Plan for photos.** For skin problems, take clear photos in daylight in case the clinician asks for them.

## Privacy and licensing

Use a secure platform provided by the clinic or service rather than a general video app. Clinicians usually need to be licensed in the place where you are located during the visit, so tell them if you are travelling.

## Costs and coverage

Many insurers and public systems cover telehealth, sometimes at a lower cost than in-person visits. Self-pay prices vary widely — ask before booking, and check whether follow-up messages are included.

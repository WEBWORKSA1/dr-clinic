Stress is the body's response to pressure. In short bursts it can sharpen focus. When it becomes constant, it affects sleep, mood, concentration, digestion and physical health. **Anxiety disorders** — the most common mental-health conditions worldwide — involve worry or fear that is persistent, out of proportion and hard to control.

## Common signs

- Feeling on edge, restless or irritable
- Racing thoughts and constant worry
- Trouble sleeping or concentrating
- A fast heartbeat, tight chest, sweating, shaking or stomach upset
- Avoiding situations that trigger worry
- Sudden waves of intense fear with physical symptoms (panic attacks)

Physical symptoms such as chest pain or breathlessness should be checked by a clinician, especially the first time — do not assume they are “just anxiety”.

## Practical techniques

- **Slow breathing.** Breathe in through your nose for about 4 seconds and out for about 6, for a few minutes. A longer out-breath calms the stress response. Try the guided exercise in our [video library](/videos/).
- **Move your body.** Regular activity, even a 10-minute walk, reduces anxiety.
- **Protect your sleep.** See our guide to [sleeping better](/health/sleep-better/).
- **Limit caffeine and alcohol**, both of which can worsen anxiety.
- **Name it and schedule it.** Write worries down and set a short “worry time” each day rather than carrying them all day.
- **Stay connected.** Talk to someone you trust; isolation makes anxiety worse.
- **Ground yourself.** Notice five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear, two you can smell and one you can taste.

## When to seek help

Talk to a clinician if anxiety lasts for weeks, affects work, study or relationships, causes panic attacks, leads you to avoid things, or if you are using alcohol or other substances to cope.

## Effective treatments

- **Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)** and related talking therapies teach skills to manage anxious thoughts and behaviours. They are available in person, by video and through some guided digital programs.
- **Medicines** such as certain antidepressants can help, often alongside therapy.
- Your primary care clinician can assess you, start treatment and refer you to a psychologist, therapist or psychiatrist.

Most appointments are short. A few minutes of preparation helps you use that time well, remember what you meant to say, and leave with a clear plan.

## Before the appointment

**Write down your main concern.** If you have several, rank them and start with the one that worries you most. Clinicians can only help with what they know about.

**Describe your symptoms.** Note when they started, how often they happen, how severe they are, what makes them better or worse, and what you have already tried. A [symptom journal](/tools/symptom-journal/) kept over a week or two is even better.

**List every medicine.** Include doses and how you take them, plus over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and herbal products — or bring the containers.

**Gather records.** Recent test results, letters from other clinicians and, if relevant, home readings such as blood pressure.

**Bring support if you want it.** A family member or friend can take notes and help you remember.

## Questions to ask

About a new problem:

- What do you think is causing this, and what else could it be?
- Do I need tests? What will they show, and how will they change what we do?
- What are my treatment options, including doing nothing for now?
- What are the benefits, risks and side effects of each option?

About a diagnosis:

- How sure are you of the diagnosis?
- What happens next, and what should I expect over time?
- Where can I find reliable information about it?

About practical next steps:

- What should I do if it gets worse, and who should I call?
- When should I come back, or when will I hear about results?
- Is this covered, and roughly what will it cost?

## During the visit

- Be honest about symptoms, habits and whether you are taking medicines as prescribed. Clinicians have heard it all.
- Ask the clinician to explain unfamiliar words, or to draw or write things down.
- If you disagree or have concerns, say so. Good care is a shared decision.
- Take notes, or ask whether a written summary is available.

## Before you leave: teach-back

Repeat the plan in your own words: “So I'll take this twice a day for a week, book a blood test, and come back if the fever lasts more than three days.” This simple step catches misunderstandings before they cause problems.

## After the visit

Collect prescriptions, book tests and follow-ups, and add any new medicines to your list or [medication schedule](/tools/medication-schedule/). If you do not hear about results when expected, ask — do not assume no news is good news.

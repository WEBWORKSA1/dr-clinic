How BMI is calculated

Body mass index is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared (kg/m²). In US units the formula is weight in pounds × 703 ÷ height in inches squared. It was designed to compare weight across large groups of people, and it remains a simple first check for individuals.

What BMI does not tell you

  • Where fat is stored. Fat around the waist carries more health risk — check your waist-to-height ratio.
  • Muscle versus fat. Athletes can have a high BMI with low body fat.
  • Children and teens, who need age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles, and pregnant people.

When to talk to a clinician

Talk to your doctor if your BMI is under 18.5 or 30 and above, if your weight has changed a lot without trying, or if you would like support with weight. A clinician can look at blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol and family history alongside BMI.

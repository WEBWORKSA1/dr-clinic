Where the numbers come from

The baseline uses adequate intakes from the US National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine: about 3.7 litres of total water a day for men, 2.7 litres for women, 3.0 litres in pregnancy and 3.8 litres while breastfeeding. We assume about 80% comes from drinks, then add roughly 0.35 litres for every 30 minutes of exercise and 0.5 litres for hot conditions.

Simple hydration habits

  • Drink with every meal and keep a refillable bottle nearby.
  • Check your urine: pale yellow usually means you are drinking enough.
  • Drink before, during and after exercise, and more in heat.
  • Older adults may feel less thirsty, so planned drinks help.

Some people need less. If you have heart failure, kidney disease or liver disease, or take medicines that affect fluid balance, follow your clinician's fluid advice. Drinking very large amounts quickly can be dangerous.

Signs of dehydration

Thirst, dark urine, dry mouth, tiredness, headache and dizziness are common. Seek care for confusion, fainting, a very fast heartbeat, no urine for 8 hours or more, or dehydration in a baby or older person.

How we work out the range

The healthy-weight range shown first is the weight that would give a body mass index between 18.5 and 24.9 at your height — the range the World Health Organization uses for adults. It is broad on purpose: people of the same height can be healthy at quite different weights.

The classic formulas

  • Devine (1974): 50 kg (men) or 45.5 kg (women) plus 2.3 kg per inch over 5 feet.
  • Robinson (1983): 52 kg (men) or 49 kg (women) plus 1.9 kg or 1.7 kg per inch over 5 feet.
  • Miller (1983): 56.2 kg (men) or 53.1 kg (women) plus 1.41 kg or 1.36 kg per inch over 5 feet.

These were created for clinical calculations, not to define how anyone should look. They are least reliable for people shorter than 5 feet (152 cm).

Better questions than “what should I weigh?”

Is your waist less than half your height? Are your blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol in healthy ranges? Can you do the activities you enjoy? Your clinician can help you look at the whole picture.

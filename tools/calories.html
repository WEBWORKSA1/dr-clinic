The formula

We use the Mifflin–St Jeor equation: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years), plus 5 for males or minus 161 for females. BMR is then multiplied by an activity factor to estimate total daily energy expenditure — the calories that would keep your weight roughly stable.

Choosing your activity level

  • Sedentary: desk job and little planned exercise.
  • Lightly active: light exercise 1–3 days a week.
  • Moderately active: moderate exercise 3–5 days a week.
  • Very active: hard exercise 6–7 days a week.
  • Extra active: a physical job plus training, or training twice a day.

Most people overestimate their activity. If unsure, choose one level lower.

Using the number sensibly

Food quality matters as much as quantity: plenty of vegetables, fruit, whole grains, beans, nuts and lean protein supports energy, fullness and long-term health. Large or rapid changes to how much you eat are best planned with a doctor or registered dietitian, especially during pregnancy or breastfeeding, for teenagers, for older adults, and for anyone with diabetes, kidney disease or a history of disordered eating.

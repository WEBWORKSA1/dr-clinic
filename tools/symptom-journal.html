Why keep a symptom journal?

Patterns are hard to remember but easy to see on paper. A journal helps you and your clinician spot triggers, judge whether a treatment is working and decide on next steps. It is especially useful for headaches and migraine, digestive symptoms, pain, allergies, sleep problems, mood and menstrual symptoms.

Tips for a useful journal

  • Record entries at the time, not from memory at the end of the week.
  • Use the same 1–10 severity scale every time.
  • Note medicines taken and whether they helped.
  • Bring the printed journal or CSV to your appointment.

A journal is for tracking, not for waiting out serious symptoms. Seek urgent care for new severe symptoms, and call your emergency number for chest pain, trouble breathing or signs of stroke.

How to read health news wisely

  1. Check who was studied. Results in cells or animals often do not carry over to people. Look for human studies, ideally large ones.
  2. Relative versus absolute risk. “Doubles the risk” can mean going from 1 in 10,000 to 2 in 10,000. Ask what the actual numbers are.
  3. One study is not the final word. Strong conclusions usually rest on several studies pointing the same way, summarised in systematic reviews.
  4. Look for who paid. Funding and conflicts of interest are usually listed in the original paper.
  5. Talk before you change treatment. Never stop or change a prescribed medicine because of a headline — ask your clinician or pharmacist first.
